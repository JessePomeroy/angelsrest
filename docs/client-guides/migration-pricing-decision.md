# Migration assistance pricing — owner decision draft

Recommendation: one standard content/media export at no charge; **$300 USD for up to three hours of scoped migration assistance**, then **$100/hour only with advance approval**. These are proposed owner prices, not a published schedule or a claim about market averages.

| Offer | Proposed price | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard content handoff | Included | One export of available tenant content/media; no destination setup, conversion or rebuild |
| Assisted handoff | $300, up to three hours | Agreed transfer/coordination/verification tasks |
| Additional assistance | $100/hour | Written estimate and approval before exceeding the allowance |
| Replacement backend, redesign or complex migration | Separate quote | Discovery first; no promise that three hours reproduces Angels Rest services |

At $100/hour, the standard allowance covers about 30 minutes of planning, 90 minutes of transfer/adaptation, and an hour of verification/handoff. This is a pricing model, not an estimate for every client. A two-hour allowance at $150/hour is an alternative if that matches the owner's usual rate; a $500 starting package may fit a five-hour migration better, but should still have a scope limit.

For Queen Worm, distinguish four different jobs: a media/content export; handing the frontend to a developer; connecting that frontend to a new backend; rebuilding on another platform. The shared private packages, tenant configuration, CMS/CRM, gallery delivery and commerce integrations make the latter jobs materially larger than copying files. Quote those after inspecting the destination and data volume.

Why separate export from paid assistance: the client can obtain their content without buying engineering time they do not need, while migration labor is priced explicitly. WordPress likewise distinguishes a content export from a full site transfer that includes design/plugins; this is a useful scope distinction, not a price benchmark: [WordPress export documentation](https://wordpress.com/support/export/)

Before publishing the client policy:

- Approve the price, included hours and additional hourly rate.
- Verify a complete tenant-scoped export/handoff procedure. There is no new self-service export button in this change; fulfillment would be operator-assisted until that tooling exists.
- Confirm the project agreement's treatment of frontend source, custom design and shared-package licensing; do not promise ownership or transfer rights not granted there.
- Use an agreed end-of-hosting date for planned departures. Offboarding immediately takes the public site offline; 90-day retention is not continued hosting.
- Align the owner-operated immediate-erasure control with any retention promise made to the client. The proposed client wording allows earlier deletion on the client's request.
- Keep financial/signed/audit retention separate from ordinary content deletion, and do not promise complete deletion of provider backups or previously downloaded copies.

The client-facing draft is `hosting-and-moving-your-website-draft.md`. Neither draft has been sent, published or incorporated into a client agreement.

See [the proposed free-export specification](basic-content-export-spec.md) for the package contents, exclusions, implementation phases and acceptance checks. The recommended first build is operator-assisted; a dashboard export button is a later decision.
